SPUR Fashion Show

SOMNIA 2026: a dream of never growing up.

My role

Head Executive of Visual Production, September 2025 to May 2026.

SOMNIA was the 14th annual Spur Fashion Show, held at Mustang Lounge on March 12, 2026, and it sold out. As head of the visual production team, I worked with the show's directors and internal teams to carry one creative vision through every shoot, film, and screen.

The visual production team: Aariz Nathani, Mateo Cannakis, Clarence Wang, Ashwin Krishan, and Simon Leone.

  • 5 promotional photoshootsTime-sensitive shoots through the year
  • Show-day livestreamMulti-cam on OBS and Zoom
  • $70,000 raisedFor Ark Aid Mission

Scene breakdown

Special thanks to our fellow creative executives, including but not limited to the makeup, hair, props, and choreography teams.

Four scenes

The show follows The Dreamer through four scenes, each with its own film. Pick a scene to change its colours.

SOMNIA

Four scenes, from a childhood daydream to waking up free.

SCENE 1

Lullaby

Nostalgia; childlike innocence; freedom.

The first of four scene interludes introduces our opening group of dancers. Inspired by childhood innocence and imagined as a nostalgic dream, the montage builds its world through colour palette, whimsical atmosphere, and playful visual storytelling. It was shot on location at Soho Studios, University Heights Public School, and London Public Library.

The Beatles’ “Strawberry Fields Forever” was the perfect song for Scene 1. Its dreamy mood and eerie lyrics echo the themes of Lullaby while hinting at the scenes still to come; a nightmare is about to disrupt the narrative.

SCENE 2

Labyrinth

A nightmare sequence; overwhelming stress.

Instead of music, we opted for an eerie soundscape to set the tone of Scene 2. Meant to contrast our first scene, Labyrinth is an evocative nightmare sequence inspired by horror symbolism.

The childhood imagery of Lullaby returns distorted: alphabet blocks dripping with paint, faces painted white, and moody lighting. Creative photography and colour complement the scene’s horror elements. Shot on location at Soho Studios and a private backyard.

Inspiration

Add your references for this scene.

SCENE 3

Havoc

Chaos: anxiety; a hectic corporate environment.

Havoc depicts the dread of corporate work culture and academia, building on the chaos of Labyrinth. Scene 3 is a chaotic manifestation of anxiety: office life at full speed, with papers flying and a desk buried in laptops and files.

Blazers, ties, collared shirts, and preppy headbands dress the cast, while high angles and crowded, fast framing keep the pressure on. Shot in black and white on a white set at Soho Studios.

Special thanks to Mateo Cannakis for videography and editing.

Inspiration

Add your references for this scene.

SCENE 4

Lucidity

Liberation; streetwear; popular culture.

The Dreamer wakes up free. Lucidity closes the show surrounded by streetwear, community, and popular culture. Denim, caps, fur trapper hats, lace, and leather fill the frame, captured on fisheye and wide lenses across snowy streets, a packed garage, and a neon bar.

The second half takes its cue from the music video for Snoop Dogg and Pharrell’s “Drop It Like It’s Hot”, with black-and-white dance takes.
